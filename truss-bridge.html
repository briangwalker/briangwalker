Closed-loop truss bridge test rig

Closed-loop truss bridge test rig
In progress

A spaghetti/balsa truss bridge, loaded not by a passive water drip but by a Raspberry-Pi-controlled pump — so the loading itself becomes a control problem. Comparing four joint types (tape, super glue, putty, string) under identical, repeatable loading instead of by feel.

Control plan for the pump loop

03Run it open-loop / bang-bang first (relay: full on/off around a force threshold) and see it chatter.Open- vs. closed-loop
04Design a PID controller against the fitted model; tune it in MATLAB, driving the pump by PWM through a MOSFET/motor-driver instead of the relay.PID control
05Simulate the closed loop and check rise time, overshoot, and settling time before touching hardware.Step response specs
06Load the tuned gains onto the Pi and compare the real response to the simulated one.Plant–sensor–actuator loop

Why this, though

“…a machine weighing 8,000 pounds demonstrated its power both to lift itself from the ground and to maintain a speed of from 30 to 40 miles per hour, but failed of success owing to the inability to balance and steer it properly. This inability to balance and steer still confronts students of the flying problem… When this one feature has been worked out, the age of flying machines will have arrived, for all other difficulties are of minor importance.” — Wilbur Wright, address to the Western Society of Engineers, Chicago, September 18, 1901

By 1901 lift and power were basically solved. What was missing was control — four years before Kitty Hawk. That's the thread running through this project: the interesting engineering problem is rarely "can it move," it's "can you make it do what you want."
